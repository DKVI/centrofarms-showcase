FROM FARM TO CUP

Grown With Purpose: The Farmers Behind Every Bean

Great coffee starts long before it reaches the roaster. It begins with the farmers, the land they care for, and the knowledge passed down through generations.

CentroFarms™ works with farming communities and family-run estates in Vietnam's highlands, including Son La and Lam Dong. These regions offer distinctive growing conditions and a long tradition of coffee cultivation.

Our sourcing approach focuses on building direct relationships with growers, improving supply chain transparency, and supporting farming practices that protect soil health over time. By connecting agricultural production with responsible soil management, we aim to build a coffee business that benefits both the people who grow the beans and the customers who bring them to market.

The Three Pillars Of Our Sourcing

01

Direct Relationships At Origin

We work to build long-term relationships with local growers, family estates, and regional cooperatives. By improving visibility across the supply chain and establishing clear purchasing arrangements, we aim to provide farmers with fair, transparent terms while giving buyers a better understanding of where their coffee comes from.

02

Healthier Soils, Better Farming Practices

We encourage soil management practices that support long-term agricultural productivity, including organic matter management, biological soil amendments, and agroforestry where suitable. These practices can help maintain soil structure, support nutrient cycling, and improve the resilience of coffee-growing systems.

03

Generational Knowledge And Local Heritage

Behind every harvest is the experience of farmers who understand their land and its growing conditions. We value that knowledge and work to preserve the traditions, skills, and regional character that make Vietnamese coffee distinctive.

Our goal is to connect this heritage with the quality standards and traceability expected by today's specialty coffee market.

TERROIR & ELEVATION

Single Origin
A Taste Of Vietnam's Mountain Highlands

Vietnam is widely recognized for its Robusta production, but its highland regions also offer exciting opportunities for specialty Arabica.

CentroFarms™ focuses on Arabica grown in selected highland areas, with target sourcing elevations of approximately 800 to 1,350 meters above sea level. Cooler temperatures at higher elevations can slow cherry maturation, giving the beans more time to develop their flavor characteristics.

Growing conditions, coffee variety, soil, harvest timing, and processing all contribute to the final cup. Depending on the lot, these factors can produce coffees with bright acidity, floral aromas, fruit-forward sweetness, or a more rounded and balanced profile.

Why Single Origin Matters

Distinctive Regional Character

Keeping lots separated by origin helps preserve the characteristics of individual farms and growing areas instead of combining them into a generic regional blend.

Greater Traceability

We aim to maintain clear sourcing records that connect each lot to its growing region and, where available, its farm or producer.

More Control Over Quality

Separating lots allows roasters to evaluate each coffee on its own merits and select beans that fit their menus, roast profiles, and customer preferences.

For CentroFarms™, single origin is about more than geography. It is a way to showcase the diversity of Vietnamese coffee and give commercial buyers greater visibility into what they are purchasing.
HARVESTING & QUALITY CONTROL

Quality Begins With Careful Selection

The quality of a finished coffee depends on decisions made at every stage, from picking the cherries to preparing the green beans for export.

CentroFarms™ emphasizes selective harvesting, appropriate post-harvest handling, and consistent quality checks to help maintain the character of each lot. Depending on the producer and processing facility, these steps may include flotation, optical sorting, controlled drying, and moisture testing.

Our Four-Stage Quality Process

01

Selective Cherry Harvesting

Where harvesting practices allow, pickers select ripe cherries at the appropriate stage of maturity. Consistent ripeness helps support more even processing and a more balanced final cup.

02

Density Sorting And Flotation

Cherries may pass through water-based flotation systems to separate lower-density fruit from denser cherries. Because floating fruit is not always defective, the results are assessed alongside other quality indicators.

03

Optical Sorting And Defect Removal

Where optical sorting equipment is available, cameras and automated rejection systems help identify cherries or beans with unwanted color variations and visible defects. This process complements, rather than replaces, careful harvesting and manual inspection.

04

Controlled Drying And Final Inspection

Coffee is dried using the method appropriate to its processing style, with raised, ventilated drying beds used where suitable. Moisture levels are monitored to support stable storage and transport.

10.5%–11.5% TARGET MOISTURE RANGE

Our target moisture range for export-ready green coffee is generally around 10.5%–11.5%, subject to the applicable buyer specifications and quality standards.

By combining careful handling with consistent checks, we aim to deliver green coffee that is traceable, properly prepared, and ready for commercial roasting.

COFFEE PROCESSING METHODS

How Processing Shapes The Flavor In Your Cup

Processing is one of the most important factors influencing the character of a coffee. After harvesting, the way the fruit is removed, fermented, and dried can significantly affect sweetness, acidity, body, and aroma.

CentroFarms™ offers different processing styles to give roasters a choice of flavor profiles and applications.

FEATURED TIER · SUMMIT MICRO-LOT

Anaerobic Fermentation

Target score: 85+ SCA points

For selected lots, whole 100% ripe cherries are fermented in sealed, low-oxygen environments, and cured slowly through raised-bed sun drying for up to 30 days. Producers monitor variables such as fermentation time and temperature to develop specific flavor characteristics.

Typical cup profile: Potentially pronounced tropical fruit, complex aromatics, spice, and distinctive fermentation-derived notes.

Best suited for: Limited releases, premium tasting menus, experimental brewing, and roasters seeking distinctive coffees.

FEATURED TIER · ESTATE NATURAL

Natural — Dry Process

Target score: 83–85 SCA points

In the natural process, whole coffee cherries are dried with the fruit intact. Drying conditions and fermentation are carefully managed to help develop the desired flavor profile.

Typical cup profile: Ripe berries, stone fruit, rounded sweetness, fuller body, and potential wine-like notes.

Best suited for: Fruit-forward single-origin espresso, specialty pour-over, and seasonal coffee menus.

FEATURED TIER · ORIGIN WASHED

Washed — Wet Process

Target score: 80–82 SCA points

In the washed process, the outer fruit is removed before the beans are fermented or otherwise processed to remove the remaining mucilage. The beans are then washed as required and dried.

Typical cup profile: Cleaner flavor definition, brighter acidity, floral or citrus notes, and a lighter, more structured body.

Best suited for: Filter coffee, light-to-medium roasts, and specialty house blends where clarity and balance are important.

Flavor outcomes vary with the coffee variety, harvest, fermentation conditions, and roasting approach. Each lot should be evaluated individually through cupping before its final profile is described.
